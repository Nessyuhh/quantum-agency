Concrete examples

Before, after

Six situations we meet in SMEs and mid-sized companies, and what changes once the flow is in place. Every time, a person keeps control of what matters.

Example 01

Supplier invoices

Accounting, administration.

Before

Invoices arrive by e-mail, as PDFs, sometimes by post. Someone opens them, re-types them into the accounting software, checks the amount, files the document. Typing errors surface at bank reconciliation.

After

The invoice is read on arrival, checked against the purchase order, entered into your software and filed. The supplier receives an acknowledgement. You get a summary every morning.

What stays human

Any invoice out of the ordinary (unexpected amount, unknown supplier) is set aside and shown to you with an explanation. You validate.

Example 02

Customer reminders

Sales, accounts receivable.

Before

Unpaid invoices are tracked in a spreadsheet. Reminders go out when someone remembers, in a tone that varies with the person and the mood of the day. Some customers are never reminded.

After

At day 7, 21 and 35, a reminder goes out automatically, in the tone you approved, with the right amount and the right invoice attached. The tracking table updates itself.

What stays human

Important or sensitive customers are flagged before sending: you decide to call rather than write. Escalating to collections remains a human decision.

Example 03

Quotes

Sales, trades, engineering offices.

Before

Every quote request takes an hour: reread the request, find the prices, lay it out, send it. Requests pile up at the end of the day and some wait several days.

After

The request is read, the elements extracted, a quote is prepared from your price list and your previous quotes, in your usual template. It waits for you, ready to review.

What stays human

Nothing leaves without your review. Atypical requests (unusual job, customer to negotiate with) are shown separately, with the points to watch.

Example 04

Recruitment

Human resources, leaders who recruit alone.

Before

Applications arrive by e-mail and on job boards. Screening happens when there is time; the best candidates have sometimes signed elsewhere by the time they are called back.

After

Each application is received, summarised in ten lines against your criteria, and ranked. Candidates get an answer the same day. You look at a short, up-to-date list with each person's strengths.

What stays human

You decide whom to meet. No automatic rejection: the decision about a person stays yours, and the screening criteria are written down and checkable.

Example 05

Customer service

Support, after-sales, front desk.

Before

The same questions come back by e-mail and phone: lead times, opening hours, order tracking, documents to send back. Genuinely complex requests wait behind the simple ones.

After

Routine requests get a precise, immediate answer, based on your own documents. Complex requests are sorted by urgency and passed to the right person, with the context already summarised.

What stays human

An unhappy customer, a complaint, an ambiguous request: these cases are detected and handed to a person, never handled by the machine alone.

Example 06

Meeting notes

Management, project leads, any team that meets.

Before

Someone takes notes, writes them up that evening or the next day, forgets a decision, sends the notes too late. Agreed actions get lost.

After

The meeting is recorded with the participants' consent. The notes are written within the hour: decisions, actions, owners, deadlines. Everyone receives theirs.

What stays human

The notes are reviewed by the facilitator before sending. Confidential topics are excluded from the recording, and recordings never leave your tools.
